# Issue #00 创刊宣言 | Manifesto

---

## 🇨🇳 中文版：你好，这里是《日常AI》
### 为什么我不关心大模型参数，只关心今晚吃什么？

> **专栏定位**：只聊生活中的 AI，记录普通人的日常实践。  
> **主编寄语**：AI 不是造火箭的专属图纸，它是普通人生活里的一把趁手瑞士军刀。

---

### 【实验背景 / The Friction】
过去一年多，科技圈每天都在被新的英文缩写和天文数字轰炸：
70B、405B、上下文窗口千万级、MMLU 跑分又破了世界纪录、某某公司又融了数亿美金……

然而，某个周二晚上 8 点，我拖着疲惫的身体站在出租屋的小厨房里，拉开冰箱门——里面躺着半截蔫掉的黄瓜、三个吃剩的番茄、半盒快过期的原味酸奶，以及一包冷冻室里冻得硬邦邦的鸡胸肉。

那一瞬间，我脑子里没有任何一个大模型的参数。我只想知道：**今晚我到底能吃什么？我还能在 20 分钟内搞定它并且不用点满是科技与狠活的外卖吗？**

我们普通人不需要 AI 帮我们证明庞加莱猜想，我们需要 AI 告诉我们这三样剩菜怎么混在一起才不会变成黑暗料理；我们不需要 AI 生成宏大的宇宙科幻史诗，我们需要它帮我们看懂母亲那份全是箭头和医学术语的体检报告，或者在深夜崩溃时把 300 条凌乱的待办琐事理成一张明天能照着做的时间表。

于是，有了《日常AI》（Everyday AI Lab）。

---

### 【旧习惯 vs 新思路 / The Shift】

* **旧习惯**：把 AI 当成一个全知全能的“考试机器”或“搜索引擎”，每次输入框里只敲几个冰冷的关键词，比如“剩菜 食谱”，然后得到一堆千篇一律、原料你根本没有的废话网络抓取结果。
* **新思路**：把 AI 当成一个坐在你厨房台面上的朋友。随手拍一张乱糟糟的冰箱隔层照片扔过去，加上一句毫无修饰的日常大白话：“就这些，20 分钟内上桌，不想洗太多锅，怎么搞？”

**从“搜答案”到“抛情境”，从“仰望参数”到“解决麻烦”——这就是《日常AI》唯一关心的进化。**

---

### 【真实互动实录 / The Conversation】

这里不搞虚构案例。这是那天晚上我和 AI 的三轮真实对话切片：

> **我**：  
> *[发送冰箱实拍原图]*  
> 冰箱里就这点库存了，今晚累得不想动脑子。请从照片里识别出还能吃的东西，给出一道 20 分钟内能搞定的热菜方案。要求：调料只用最基础的油盐酱油，尽量少洗一个锅。
>
> **AI**：  
> *识别到：半截黄瓜、3 个番茄、1 块冷冻鸡胸肉、半盒原味酸奶。*  
> 建议放弃传统的炒鸡胸肉（因为解冻需要 15 分钟，且容易柴）。今晚做**【茄汁酸奶嫩鸡丁烩饭】**，只需一口平底锅：  
> 1. 鸡胸肉无需完全解冻，趁硬直接切薄片（省时 10 分钟），用两勺酸奶和一撮盐抓匀腌制 3 分钟（酸奶中的乳酸能瞬间软化鸡肉纤维，比淀粉嫩 10 倍）；  
> 2. 番茄切碎直接下锅炒出沙，加少许水；  
> 3. 下入鸡片和黄瓜丁焖煮 3 分钟，直接把剩米饭倒进去吸饱汤汁出锅。全程用时 14 分钟，洗一个锅。

你看，没有人提到 Transformer 架构，但那顿酸奶腌制的茄汁鸡肉饭，治愈了我周二夜晚所有的疲惫与焦虑。

---

### 【可抄作业的模板 / The Takeaway】

如果今晚你也站在冰箱前发愁，直接复制这个提示词框架：

```markdown
【日常提问框架：生活琐事即时破局】
背景现状：[拍照上传，或用语音输入当前拥有的物资/条件]
核心约束：时间限制在 [XX] 分钟以内 / 预算不超过 [XX] / 工具只有 [平底锅/微波炉/手机]
终极目标：请帮我制定一个 [低心智负担/步骤最少] 的解决方案，并把第一步拆解到 1 分钟内就能动手。
```

> 💡 **主编习惯感悟**：  
> **真正改变生活的不是 AI 的智商，而是你向它敞开生活具体细节的坦诚度。**

## 🇺🇸 English Version: Welcome to Everyday AI Lab
### Why I Stopped Caring About Benchmarks and Started Fixing My Dinner

> **Mission**: Only about AI in real life. Practical experiments for everyday humans.  
> **Editor's Note**: AI is not a blueprint for rocket scientists; it is a pocket-sized Swiss Army knife for ordinary people.

---

### 【The Friction】
Every morning, my feed is carpet-bombed with astronomical numbers and obscure acronyms: 70B, 405B, million-token context windows, new benchmark records, and another ten-digit funding round.

Yet, on a chilly Tuesday at 8 PM, I found myself standing in front of my tiny kitchen fridge, staring blankly inside. 

What did I have? Half a withered cucumber, three bruised tomatoes, half a tub of plain yogurt on the brink of expiration, and a rock-solid chicken breast buried deep in the freezer.

At that exact second, I couldn't care less about benchmark scores or transformer architectures. The only thing echoing in my head was: **What on earth am I eating tonight? Can I get dinner on the table in under 20 minutes without ordering greasy takeout?**

Most of us don't need AI to prove Poincaré's conjecture. We need it to figure out how three random leftovers in the crisper drawer can turn into an edible meal; we need it to translate a bewildering medical report for our aging parents without terrifying them; and on frantic Sunday nights, we need it to untangle a chaotic mess of 30 errands into a clear, doable Monday roadmap.

That is why **Everyday AI Lab** exists.

---

### 【The Shift】

* **The Old Reflex**: Treating AI like an intimidating exam engine or a rigid search bar. You punch in cold keywords like *"leftover recipes"* and get five sponsored links and a 2,000-word blog post about someone’s grandmother in Tuscany before ever seeing the ingredients.
* **The New Reflex**: Treating AI like a smart, relaxed friend perched on your kitchen counter. You snap a photo of the messy fridge shelf and say: *"Here’s the mess. 20 minutes. Minimal dishes. What’s the play?"*

**From searching for generic answers to sharing real-life contexts; from obsessing over parameters to fixing everyday headaches. That is the only evolution we care about here.**

---

### 【The Conversation】

No hypothetical corporate pitch decks. Here is what actually went down on Tuesday night:

> **Me**:  
> *[Attached raw photo of the messy fridge]*  
> Here is everything I have left. I am too drained to think. Identify what is usable, and give me a hot meal ready in under 20 minutes. Keep seasonings to basic salt and soy sauce, and use as few pans as humanly possible.
>
> **AI**:  
> *Identified: Half cucumber, 3 tomatoes, 1 frozen chicken breast, plain yogurt.*  
> Forget traditional thawing—it takes 15 minutes and ends up dry. Let’s make **One-Pan Tomato & Yogurt Glazed Chicken Rice**:  
> 1. Slice the chicken while still semi-frozen (saves 10 mins). Toss it with two tablespoons of yogurt and a pinch of salt for 3 minutes. The lactic acid tenderizes the meat faster than cornstarch ever could.  
> 2. Dice tomatoes and cook them in a skillet until they break down into a sauce.  
> 3. Drop in the chicken slices and cucumber, simmer for 3 minutes, then fold in cold rice to soak up the sauce.  
> Total time: 14 minutes. Exactly one skillet to wash.

It took zero prompt engineering jargon, but that simple dinner saved my evening.

---

### 【The Takeaway】

Next time life throws you a chaotic curveball, steal this everyday prompt:

```markdown
【The Everyday Life-Fixer Framework】
Current Context: [Snap a photo, or voice-rant your current raw ingredients/situation]
Hard Constraints: Done in under [XX] minutes / Zero budget / Only have [a skillet/microwave/phone]
Outcome: Give me a low-friction, dead-simple action plan where Step 1 takes under 60 seconds to start.
```

> 💡 **Habit Shift**:  
> **What transforms your life isn't the model's IQ—it's how candidly you invite it into your messy, everyday reality.**
